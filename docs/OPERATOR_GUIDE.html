🎬 Reel Maker — Team Guide

Turn a short written story + your photos and clips into a finished, captioned vertical video — no editing skills needed. This guide explains every button and field in plain language, with the exact words you can type.

🛍 Doing a brand deal or ad? Use the Brand / Ad mode link at the top. It works the same way, plus: you fill a short brand brief (or paste the brand's brief), upload the logo and real product photos (the tool never fakes a product or logo), tick 🛍 Product beat on the product shots, and choose the voice-over and music (AI draft or the brand's own files). It won't let you render until you've added the logo, a call-to-action, and a product shot — and it auto-adds an end-card + a "Paid partnership" line. You type all the ad wording and claims yourself — the AI never writes claims.
Where to open the tool: https://creative.kevat.ai
Works in any browser. Nothing to install. If a teammate is mid-render, just wait a moment and refresh.

1 · What it does (in one minute)

You give it a story written as short lines and a folder of photos/videos. For each line it decides what should be on screen, how the camera moves, and the mood — then builds one 9:16 vertical video with captions and music, ready for Reels / Shorts.

Think of it as an automated video editor with a film director's brain. Each line of your story becomes one shot. A 10-line story becomes a 10-shot reel.

More than one person in the story? When a line quotes someone — e.g. "My son asked, 'Mom, where is father gone?'" — the tool spots it and shows that person (the kid's face, the right age) and, if lip-sync or voice-over is on, their voice — not the narrator's. 4 · Step 2 — Write the story

The story is plain text. Start each shot with the word Frame and a number, then write the line that should appear on screen. That's it.

Frame 3

Caption:
Write your full Instagram caption here. (This does NOT appear in the video.)
RulePlain meaning
One Frame = one shotKeep each line short — it shows on screen as a caption while the camera moves.
Numbers don't need to be in orderThe tool reads top-to-bottom, not the numbers.
The Caption: block at the bottomIs your Instagram post text. It is never shown in the video — write the long version here.
Keep on-screen lines under ~15 wordsThe camera moves before longer text can be read.
You don't have to type anything fancy. Everything below (picture, mood, camera, lip-sync) can be set by clicking on the frame card after you parse. The bracket tags shown later are just the typed shortcut for the same buttons.

Adding your own photos & videos

Click 📁 Browse folder… (next to the script box) and pick the folder on your computer that holds the photos/videos for this story. The tool uploads them and matches the right one to each shot automatically.

  • Put all the media for one story in a single folder first, then browse to it.
  • Supported: JPG, PNG, HEIC, WEBP images and MP4, MOV, M4V, WEBM videos.
  • The folder browser works on any device (phone/tablet/desktop) — no file size limit.
  • You can still change the picture on any individual shot afterwards (see Picture source below).
  • No folder? Leave it empty and the tool will create every visual with AI.
Tick 🤖 Smart-match after uploading and the tool reads what's in each photo (people, places, even names/text) and places the best photo on each line — instead of just going in order.
Tick 🎞 Multi-shot coverage to make longer beats richer: instead of one photo for the whole line, the tool covers it with the main shot plus 1–2 supporting B-roll stills (it picks ones that also fit that line), played as quick sub-shots with gentle camera motion under the same caption.

So a 9-second beat might become three 3-second clips (main photo + two B-roll stills), making the video feel more edited and less like a slideshow.

Note: it only kicks in when a beat is long enough and you have spare matching photos. It uses extra credits (~1.5–2× on the beats it covers), so turn it on when you want that polished, multi-cut feel for important story moments.

5 · The Frame Card — every field explained

After Parse Frames →, each line becomes a card. Top to bottom you choose: the picture, then optionally a mood note, a photo edit, a camera move, a lip-sync toggle, and the length.

💡 Suggestion chips. Under the mood note, photo edit, and camera boxes you'll see a few small clickable suggestions the tool made for that line. Click one to drop it in — then edit the words however you like, or ignore them and type your own. They're just there so you never stare at an empty box.

One move per shot. Keep it to a single gentle action (e.g. slow push in). Over-describing motion is the #1 cause of weird, melty faces. And remember: a shot is either a camera move or a talking face (lip sync) — not both.

5d · Image Edit — change a photo with words

The ✏️ Image edit box changes the picture before it animates. Type a plain instruction.

Type thisResult

Big mood changes ("add storm", "warmer light") work great. Precise moves ("shift her to the left") are less reliable — that's expected.

5e · Lip Sync — make the subject speak

Tick 🎙 Lip Sync and that shot becomes a talking face that says the line aloud in a real voice. A voice menu appears — pick one or leave the default.

  • What gets spoken = the line you wrote for that frame.
  • Length is automatic — the shot lasts exactly as long as the spoken line (the duration box greys out).
  • Music ducks under the voice automatically.
  • Safe: if a voice/lip-sync service is unavailable, the shot quietly becomes a normal animated photo. Nothing breaks.
Use it on 2–4 shots max — the spoken turning point and the closing line. Don't lip-sync everything; mix talking beats with silent cinematic beats.

5f · Voice-Over — narration over the full video

Check ☑ Voice-Over Track at the top to make the entire video narrated. Each caption gets read aloud in a chosen voice, creating a full voice-over narration layered under the music.

  • Every caption is spoken — each line reads exactly as long as the shot lasts, no gaps.
  • Voice menu — same voices as Lip Sync; pick one or use the default.
  • Plays over music — narration sits on top; music stays at 25% volume behind the voice.
  • No lip-sync — the voice is heard but faces don't move. Use this for cinematic storytelling, not dialogue.
  • Safe: if narration fails, the video still renders with just music.
Lip Sync vs Voice-Over: Use Lip Sync (☑ on a frame) when you want a character speaking on screen, moving their lips. Use Voice-Over Track when you want a narrator telling the story over cinematic shots — think documentary or portfolio reel.

8 · The 💰 cost panel — read it before Generate

After you parse, a cost estimate appears and updates live as you change settings. Check it before pressing Generate. Want it cheaper? Use Dev tier, prefer real photos/videos, and keep lip-sync to a few shots.

8b · Iterate without re-rendering — redo, approve, live clips

You don't have to render the whole video and hope. Treat the tool as a draft machine you steer: preview the stills, fix the frames you don't like, approve the rest, and only pay to animate what you've approved. Three controls make this fast.

The recommended 3-pass workflow
1. 👁 Preview Stills (free) — see every image.
2. Fix & approve per frame — 🔄 redo any image you don't like; untick frames you're not happy with yet.
3. ▶ Generate in Dev — only approved frames get paid animation; you watch each clip appear live.

🔄 Redo still — fix one image, not the whole video

After Preview Stills, each frame card shows a 🔄 Redo still button. Change that frame's note, photo, image edit, or camera — then click 🔄. Only that one image regenerates (a few seconds), and it swaps into the card. Nothing else is touched, and you don't pay to re-animate anything.

  • Use it to dial in a face, a mood, or a symbolic shot until that single frame is right.
  • Unchanged frames are never re-touched, so it's cheap and fast.
  • When the still looks right, it's the exact image that gets animated later.

✓ Approve for animation — only pay for the frames you like

Each frame card has a ✓ Approved for animation tick (on by default). Untick any frame you're not happy with. When you press Generate:

  • Approved frames → full AI animation (Kling/Higgsfield) — the good stuff, costs credits.
  • Unticked frames → a free, simple Ken Burns zoom — so the video still assembles complete and you can see the whole cut.
  • The 💰 cost panel drops as you untick frames, so you see the saving immediately.
This is the cheap way to test direction: approve only 2–3 hero frames first, render, see how they feel, then come back and approve more. You're never forced to pay for all frames at once.

🎬 Live clip reveal — watch it build

While a render runs, each frame's clip appears in its card the moment it's done — you don't wait for the whole video. The card shows ⏳ animating…, then flips to a looping 🎬 clip ready preview. If an early clip looks wrong you can stop, fix that frame with 🔄, untick it, and re-run — without having waited for all ten.

9 · How to build a story that lands

ShotJob
Frame 1Hook — who is this, why watch? Use your best clip or a crash-zoom.
2–3Context — the before.
4–5Conflict — the problem / loss (use AI Symbolic for pain).
6–7Lowest point, then the spark (great lip-sync moment).
8–9Turning point → triumph (real footage of the win; save 360 orbit for here).
10Resolution — strong, present-day, direct gaze (great closing lip-sync line).
  • 10–12 frames = a great 60–90s reel.
  • Write captions in first person, present tense, punchy, under 15 words.
  • Never show illness/trauma on a face — use AI Symbolic (the empty room speaks louder).
  • Save big moves (360 orbit, crane up) for the single biggest moment.

13 · Brand / Ad Mode — full walkthrough

Click 🛍 Brand / Ad mode in the header (or go to /brand) to switch. The engine is identical — same pipeline, same frame cards, same models — but the UI gains a brand brief panel, real-asset uploads, mandatory checks, and a product beat toggle per shot.

Iron rule: you type every word of ad copy yourself. The AI never writes, rephrases, or invents marketing claims, taglines, or on-screen text. It only makes visuals. Paste the brand's exact words into the fields below.
1
Paste the brand brief (optional) → click Extract fields →. Fields fill in with the brand's exact words — never paraphrased. Only empty fields are filled; anything you've already typed is left alone.
2
Upload the logo (required) and optionally product photos/videos.
3
Paste the ad script in the beats box, then Parse Frames →. Each beat = one frame card. Tick 🛍 Product beat on the shot(s) where the real product/logo appears.
4
Choose audio — AI announcer (draft) or brand-supplied VO file; AI music or brand-supplied cleared music.
5
👁 Preview Stills first (free — no animation) to check the look and product-beat frames.
6
▶ Generate Ad — the tool checks all mandatories before spending any credits and shows a checklist if anything is missing.

13c · Audio — VO & music

Brand mode separates the announcer voice-over from the background music. Both are decided per-project.

13d · Writing the ad script & marking product beats

Write the script exactly like a story script — one caption per Frame. The caption is the on-screen text the brand supplies. After parsing, each frame card gets a 🛍 Product beat toggle.

Beat typeWhat to do
Lifestyle / story beatWrite the brand-supplied line. Leave product beat off. The AI makes the visual.
Product / logo shotTick 🛍 Product beat. Upload a real product photo in the Brand kit. That photo is used as-is — no AI generation.
End-card beatAuto-generated by the tool from your CTA text, logo, and brand colour. You don't write it — it's appended automatically.
Best structure for a 15–30s ad:
Frame 1: Lifestyle hook (relatable scene, brand-neutral)
Frame 2–3: Problem or desire
Frame 4: Product introduction (🛍 Product beat — your product photo)
Frame 5: Key message (brand's exact words on screen)
End-card: Auto-generated CTA

13e · Mandatories hard-block (the checklist)

If you click Generate Ad with something missing, the tool refuses to spend any credits and shows a red checklist. You cannot generate until all boxes are ticked. This protects against accidentally publishing an ad without a logo or disclosure.
