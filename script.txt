Caption 
“As a child, I didn’t know what fashion meant, but I knew colours. The pinks, the blues, the oranges, they spoke to me. Art was my escape, my way of expressing what I couldn’t say out loud. My mother would take me to art competitions across Delhi, cheering me on even when I didn’t win. That’s where it all began — with crayons, craft paper, and my mom’s faith.
When I got into NIFT, I stitched till midnight, poured my heart into fabrics, and dreamt in patterns. Slowly, I built my life around fashion. From working with fashion brands in Mumbai to heading multiple brands and building my own label, my work has always been loud and proud.
But when I began illustrating my reality — as a queer, turbaned Sikh man, things turned against me. In 2018, I was threatened, attacked, made to apologise publicly, and forced to take down my work. Online too, the trolling was relentless. ‘Chakka.’ ‘Ladka hoke make-up karta hai.’ But for me, my sexuality is as much a part of my identity as my religion. It took a toll on me but even more on my family. They’d often worry, asking, ‘Why do you always have to stay so close to danger?’
People even asked me to cut my hair and renounce my religion so that I could continue with my work. But my sexuality is as much a part of my identity as my faith. Even today, I face hate. My studio has been vandalised. I’ve been assaulted. And still I create. Because silence never protects anyone and I am not the one to stop — my work is my armour. Growing up without any LGBTQ role models or safe conversations about sexuality was tough. That’s why I hope my work inspires young queer artists today — to dream louder, and to know they’re not alone.”
